GIS & ArcGIS StoryMaps

Using maps, research, and visual storytelling to investigate the movement of people, objects, ideas, and cultures.

 

What is a Geographic Information Systems (GIS)?

A Geographic Information System (GIS) is a digital tool that enables researchers to analyze information geographically. For art historians, GIS can help us investigate where people, objects, buildings, ideas, and cultural practices were located and how they moved across regions and over time.

Within the Digital Mediterranean Project, GIS encourages you to conceptualize the medieval Mediterranean spatially by considering questions such as: What regions were connected? Who migrated? What objects or ideas circulated? Where did artistic and cultural exchanges take place?

 

What is ArcGIS StoryMaps?

ArcGIS StoryMaps integrates interactive maps with text, images, video, and other multimedia elements. This digital tool enables you to present research as a visual narrative, expanding beyond the format of a traditional research paper.

GIS supports the investigation of spatial relationships, while StoryMaps provide tools to communicate your findings. To simplify the concept:

Research → GIS → Spatial Analysis → StoryMaps → Digital Presentation

 

Using StoryMaps in Academia

Using ArcGIS StoryMaps in coursework builds familiarity with digital tools, fosters critical thinking, and develops design skills. For example, students researching the medieval Mediterranean can present findings on art, architecture, and material culture in a visually appealing narrative. This approach encourages spatial thinking, digital literacy, and narrative design, and helps students share their work with a wider audience through digital platforms.

Incorporating ArcGIS StoryMaps into instruction strengthens subject mastery and practical skills. Students use StoryMaps to synthesize research on art, architecture, and material culture in compelling visual formats. This method fosters spatial awareness, digital fluency, and storytelling skills, enabling students to share their work with a wider audience.

Participation in this project encourages students to serve as investigators and storytellers. Students formulate research questions (a central argument) to demonstrate the movement of people, texts, ideas, and objects in the medieval Mediterranean world and design their StoryMaps to present their findings as a final class project. This integration of research and historical narrative skills supports their academic and professional development through the use of digital technologies and methods.

 

Pedagogical Value of Using Digital Tools

StoryMaps assignments prepare students to function as researchers and narrators, a combination valued in academic and professional development. For example, museums, cultural institutions, media outlets, and research organizations today use various digital platforms such as StoryMaps to engage with the public and share knowledge. Completing a StoryMap project provides valuable experience in digital scholarship and results in a polished, public-facing artifact suitable for portfolios, graduate school applications, or job searches.
